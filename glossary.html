Appendix B — Glossary

Author

Stefano Polla

Published

25 October 2026

Last updated

25 October 2026

calibration
Measuring and adjusting the control pulses of a device so that its gates do what they should. Providers publish calibration data: the error rates of each qubit and gate at the last calibration.
coherence time
How long a qubit keeps its quantum state, in particular a superposition, before noise from its surroundings destroys it.
coherent error
A systematic error in which a gate applies the wrong unitary, for example a rotation by a slightly wrong angle, in the same way every time.
coupler
A circuit element between two superconducting qubits through which they interact. A tunable coupler can switch the interaction on and off.
coupling graph
Graph whose vertices are qubits and whose edges are the pairs on which a native two-qubit gate is available.
cross-resonance
A two-qubit gate mechanism for superconducting qubits with fixed couplings: one qubit is driven at the frequency of its neighbour.
crosstalk
An unwanted effect of an operation on one qubit on other qubits, for example through couplings that are never fully switched off.
dephasing
Loss of the relative phase of a superposition without loss of energy, for example because the qubit frequency is shifted by an unknown amount. The measured times T2* and T2 include both dephasing and relaxation; the time of pure dephasing alone is called Tφ.
depth
The number of layers of gates in a circuit that must run one after another. Gates on different qubits can share a layer.
digital quantum computer
A quantum computer that runs circuits: sequences of gates from a fixed universal set, applied to qubits and followed by measurements. Also called gate-based, as opposed to analog devices that imitate the time evolution of a physical system directly.
dilution refrigerator
A refrigerator that reaches about 10 millikelvin, cold enough that heat no longer excites superconducting qubits.
dispersive readout
Readout of a superconducting qubit through a resonator whose frequency depends slightly on the qubit state. A weak microwave probe tells the two states apart.
DiVincenzo criteria
A checklist for building a quantum computer: scalable, well-characterised qubits; preparation of a known state; long coherence; a universal set of gates; measurement of individual qubits.
error mitigation
Methods that reduce the effect of noise on estimated quantities without correcting the errors, typically by running extra or modified circuits and combining their results. They cost extra shots.
estimator
A rule that turns measured data into an estimate of a quantity, for example the sample mean of the outcomes as an estimate of an expectation value.
fidelity
A number between 0 and 1 measuring how close a state or operation is to the intended one. The state fidelity is the probability that a state passes a test for the target state; the average gate fidelity averages it over all input states. Informally, one minus the probability of an error.
ion
An atom with one or more electrons removed, so that it carries an electric charge and can be held by electric fields.
ion trap
A device that holds ions in vacuum with electric fields, typically lined up in a chain a few micrometres apart.
Josephson junction
A thin insulating barrier between two superconductors. In a circuit it makes the energy levels unevenly spaced, which is what turns the circuit into a usable qubit.
laser cooling
Slowing the motion of atoms or ions with laser light until they are almost at rest.
leakage
An error in which a qubit leaves its two qubit levels, for example when a transmon is pushed into its third level.
logical qubit
A qubit of information encoded in many physical qubits by an error-correcting code. Its error rate can be much lower than that of the physical qubits.
Mølmer-Sørensen gate
The standard two-qubit gate of trapped ions. Laser beams couple two ions to the shared vibrations of the chain, producing an entangling rotation of any chosen angle.
native gate
A gate the hardware implements directly with a calibrated control pulse. Every other gate must be compiled into native gates.
NISQ
Noisy intermediate-scale quantum: devices with tens to thousands of qubits whose errors are not corrected, so noise limits the size of the circuits they can run.
optical tweezers
Tightly focused laser beams that hold single neutral atoms in place and can move them.
Pauli string
A tensor product of single-qubit Pauli matrices (I, X, Y or Z), one for each qubit, such as ZZ or XIY. Hermitian, and squares to the identity.
phonon
A quantum of vibration. In an ion trap, phonons are the quanta of the vibrations that the whole chain of ions shares; in a solid, such as a qubit chip, they are the quanta of the vibrations of the crystal.
phonon bus
The shared vibrations of a chain of trapped ions, used as a common channel through which any two ions can interact in a two-qubit gate.
physical qubit
A qubit as implemented in hardware, for example one transmon or one ion, with all its imperfections.
pulse
A short burst of microwave or laser radiation at a chosen frequency, switched on and off smoothly, used to drive a qubit. Its frequency selects which qubit responds; its area and phase set the rotation.
QCCD
Quantum charge-coupled device: a trapped-ion architecture in which ions are moved between zones of the trap, so that any two ions can be brought together for a gate.
quantum dot
A tiny region in a semiconductor that can trap single electrons.
quantum error correction
Encoding quantum information redundantly in many physical qubits, so that errors can be detected and corrected without disturbing the encoded information.
relaxation
Loss of energy by a qubit: the excited state decays to the ground state. Its characteristic time is called T1, and the amplitude-damping channel describes it.
resonance
Radiation drives a transition between two energy levels only when its frequency matches their energy difference divided by Planck’s constant. This is how individual qubits are addressed.
Rydberg state
A state of an atom in which one electron is excited very far from the nucleus. Atoms in Rydberg states interact strongly over micrometre distances.
shot
One run of a circuit: prepare the qubits, apply the gates, measure. Returns one bit string.
spin
An intrinsic two-level property of an electron (up or down), which can serve as a qubit.
SWAP
A two-qubit gate that exchanges the states of two qubits. It costs three CNOTs.
threshold
The physical error rate per operation below which error correction helps: below it, larger codes give lower logical error rates.
transmon
The most common superconducting qubit: a small circuit with a Josephson junction, whose two lowest energy levels form the qubit.
transpilation
Compilation of a circuit into the native gates and coupling graph of a device, including inserting SWAPs and simplifying.
virtual Z gate
A Z rotation implemented by shifting the phase of all later drive pulses instead of applying a pulse. It takes no time and adds no error.
